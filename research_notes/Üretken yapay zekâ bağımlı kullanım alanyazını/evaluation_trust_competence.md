## B1. Do students verify ChatGPT outputs, and do they accept errors uncritically? (empirical, 2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Experimental and process evidence converges on three points: students often accept wrong GenAI output as correct; using GenAI does not by itself reduce the incorporation of wrong content; and corroborating outputs against outside sources marks the stronger performers. Metacognitive accuracy and epistemic beliefs moderate how many errors students take in.

### Cited Findings
- **Krupp et al. (2024), HHAI 2024. VERIFIED.** Physics students solved exercises with a search engine (N = 12) or with unrestricted ChatGPT (N = 27). Nearly half of the ChatGPT-supported solutions were **wrongly assumed correct** by students, even within their own field. The authors call this "unreflected acceptance". Small sample — [IOS Press/SAGE DOI page](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/FAIA240195); [arXiv preprint 2309.03087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03087).
- **Urban et al. (2025), BJET 56(5), 1897–1918. VERIFIED (Wiley full text).** Experiment: 49 students wrote independently and 49 wrote with ChatGPT-4o, using an expert text (all correct) and a ChatGPT text (mixed correct and incorrect). ChatGPT assistance increased the inclusion of correct information (d = 0.64 from the expert text; d = 0.95 from the ChatGPT text) but **did not reduce the inclusion of incorrect ChatGPT content**. Higher metacognitive bias (R² = 10%) and epistemic beliefs (R² = 12%) were associated with including incorrect content. Counterintuitively, students who reported a higher need to evaluate ChatGPT knowledge included *more* inaccuracies. The practitioner notes say GenAI is treated as an "epistemic authority" because of its "authoritative tone and human-like interaction" — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13591).
- **Stadler, Bannert & Sailer (2024), Computers in Human Behavior 160, 108386. VERIFIED.** 91 university students were randomly assigned to ChatGPT-3.5 or Google for a socio-scientific inquiry task. The LLM group reported lower extraneous, intrinsic and germane load but gave **weaker justifications** — "cognitive ease at a cost" — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563224002541); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2024.108386).
- **Lehtinen, Channa, Näykki, Ahlström & Hiljanen (2026), JCAL 42(3). VERIFIED (Wiley).** 75 pre-service teachers co-created lesson plans with GenAI; the study combined video analysis and process mining. High-performing groups iterated their writing and **corroborated GenAI content with other online resources**. Low-performing groups showed repetitive prompting and reliance on AI content. The authors recommend verifying GenAI information against other sources — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70256).
- **Walton et al. (2025), AEHE, online 13 Oct 2025. PARTLY VERIFIED** (title, DOI and date confirmed; full author list not confirmed; a ResearchGate copy sits on David Boud's profile). The study identifies six kinds of "judgement events", including "adopting ideas with low levels of criticality", "misjudging GenAI contributions as their own", and "submitting GenAI content in an assignment without judging it" — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2025.2570328).
- **Darvishi, Khosravi, Sadiq, Gašević & Siemens (2024), Computers & Education 210, 104967. VERIFIED.** 1,625 students across ten courses. Students tended to **rely on rather than learn from** AI prompts. When the AI support was removed, self-regulated scaffolds helped only partly — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131523002440); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104967).

### Inferences
- For the claim that uncritical acceptance of GenAI output is documented beyond self-report, a strong cluster is (Krupp vd., 2024; Lehtinen vd., 2026; Stadler vd., 2024; Urban vd., 2025; Walton vd., 2025).
- Urban et al.'s finding that "need to evaluate" was linked to more errors supports the drafts' argument (v35 L37; v39 L189) that a general "kontrol ediyorum" statement does not show that verification was effective.

## B2. Overreliance/automation bias with LLMs, and how fluent, confident LLM language affects trust

### Takeaway
People overestimate LLM accuracy under default explanations. Longer explanations raise confidence without raising accuracy. First-person uncertainty statements reduce overreliance. Together with Alter & Oppenheimer's (2009) point that fluency serves as a truth cue, this gives a firm, multi-source basis for the K21 "düzgün cümleler" interpretation.

### Cited Findings
- **Steyvers et al. (2025), Nature Machine Intelligence 7, 221–231. VERIFIED.** The paper reports a *calibration gap* (human confidence in LLM answers versus the model's own confidence) and a *discrimination gap*. With default explanations, users **overestimate** LLM accuracy. **Longer explanations increased user confidence even when they did not improve accuracy**. Explanations adjusted to the model's internal confidence narrowed both gaps — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00976-7); [arXiv preprint version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13835).
- **Kim, Liao, Vorvoreanu, Ballard & Wortman Vaughan (2024), FAccT '24, pp. 822–835. ## B3. Evaluative judgement and the "evaluate" dimension of AI literacy in higher and teacher education with GenAI

### Takeaway
The evaluative-judgement strand (Tai et al., 2018 → Bearman & Ajjawi, 2023 → Bearman et al., 2024 → Walton et al., 2025) runs from theory to empirical "judgement events". In teacher education, empirical work shows that pre-service teachers need, and can partly show, critical evaluation of GenAI lesson materials.

### Cited Findings
- **Bearman & Ajjawi (2023), BJET 54(5), 1160–1173. VERIFIED (Wiley).** The paper treats AI as a "black box" to work *with*, not to see inside. Its pedagogy orients students to the quality standards around AI and gives them meaningful AI interactions, which "helps build evaluative judgement in weighting AI's contribution to work". It suggests asking students "When and why do they trust its output?" — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13337).
- **Long & Magerko (2020), CHI '20. VERIFIED.** It defines AI literacy as a set of competencies for users to "effectively interact with and **critically evaluate** AI". Best Paper Honorable Mention — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3313831.3376727).
- **Kalenda, Rath, Abugasea Heidt & Wright (2025), SAGE (ISSN 0047-2395, Journal of Educational Technology Systems). PARTLY VERIFIED** (volume and issue not seen). In a guided analysis, 59 pre-service education students examined ChatGPT-generated lesson plans and found missing details and inaccurate information — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00472395241301388).
- **Lehtinen et al. (2026)**: pre-service teachers who corroborated GenAI content with other sources produced higher-quality lesson plans (see B1) — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70256).
- **Walton et al. (2025)**: six categories of students' judgement events with GenAI (see B1) — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2025.2570328).
- **Su, Hsu & González Campos (2026), JCAL 42(3). VERIFIED (Wiley).** A systematic review of 54 studies of ChatGPT in mathematics education; 24.1% were set in teacher education. Accuracy and reliability concerns appeared in **46.3%** of the studies. The authors recommend making verification a core practice: tasks that require justification and correction of AI outputs — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70241).
- **Naidoo, Tai & Penman (2021; online 2020), The Clinical Teacher 18(2), 115–120. VERIFIED (Wiley).** The paper sets out the two components of evaluative judgement: contextual understanding of quality, and judging specific instances — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.13268).

### Inferences
- Suggested cluster for the teacher-education recommendation (v39 L195; v35 L197/L200): (Bearman & Ajjawi, 2023; Bearman vd., 2024; Lehtinen vd., 2026; Tai vd., 2018; Walton vd., 2025).
- Suggested cluster for the AI-literacy "evaluate" dimension: (Long & Magerko, 2020; Ng vd., 2021; Aksoy vd., 2026 [B5]).

## B4. GenAI and students' self-efficacy, perceived competence, and deskilling/skill decay

### Takeaway
There is evidence that lower academic self-efficacy predicts AI dependency, that dependency may inflate "false" self-efficacy, and that AI support can produce performance without learning. Most of the evidence is cross-sectional or conceptual. Longitudinal evidence on *perceived* competence erosion is scarce, which supports the drafts' careful separation of perception from skill.

### Cited Findings
- **Zhang, Zhao, Zhou & Kim (2024), IJETHE 21, Article 34. VERIFIED.** 300 university students; I-PACE model. The relation between academic self-efficacy and AI (ChatGPT) dependency was mediated by academic stress and performance expectations. The negative effects of dependency students reported most often included laziness, misinformation, lower creativity, and reduced critical and independent thinking — [Springer PDF listing](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s41239-024-00467-0.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-024-00467-0). (v35 L175 cites "Zhang vd., 2024"; check that it is this paper.)
- **Lee et al. (2025)**: higher *self-confidence* was associated with more critical thinking (see A4) — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/).
- **Darvishi et al. (2024)**: students relied on AI rather than learning from it, and did worse when it was removed (see B1) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104967).
- **Ganuthula, Singh & Chakraborty (2026), Human Behavior and Emerging Technologies 2026(1). VERIFIED (Wiley).** A theoretical and mathematical model of AI-induced skill atrophy (the "Paradox of Augmentation"). Task performance rises while human skill stalls or declines, a pattern the authors call a "performance mirage". The simulations suggest intermittent no-AI practice preserves skill. This is a model, not empirical data — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1155/hbe2/8303770).
- **Habib & Gardiner (2026)**: GenAI can raise creative self-efficacy but disrupt agency and ownership (see A10) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1871187126001094).
- **"The paradox of self-efficacy and technological dependence: Unraveling generative AI's impact on university students' task completion", *The Internet and Higher Education* (2025), PII S109675162400040X. UNVERIFIED METADATA** (authors, volume and DOI not seen). According to the search-surfaced abstract, GenAI dependency positively predicted **false self-efficacy** and negatively predicted achievement, "exhibiting a significant Dunning–Kruger effect". Very relevant to dimension 4, but the metadata must be confirmed first — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S109675162400040X).
- **"The relationship between academic self-efficacy and GenAI dependence among college students: a chain mediation model of academic stress and perceived usefulness of GenAI", *Frontiers in Psychology* (2026). PARTLY VERIFIED** (DOI and PubMed ID visible; authors not seen). Lower academic self-efficacy was associated with greater GenAI dependence, directly and through stress and perceived usefulness — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1784354); [PubMed 42499594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42499594/).

### Inferences
- Suggested cluster for the dimension-4 framing (perceived competence ≠ measured skill): (Fisher vd., 2015; Lee vd., 2025; Macnamara vd., 2024; Zhang vd., 2024). Add the Internet & Higher Education 2025 paper once verified.
- Suggested cluster for AI support producing performance without learning: (Bastani vd., 2025; Darvishi vd., 2024; Fan vd., 2025; Macnamara vd., 2024). Ganuthula vd. (2026) can be added, labelled as a theoretical model.
- The drafts' caution (v39 L190: "Zorlanma veya yetersizlik bildirimi, gerçek bir beceri kaybını göstermez") agrees with this literature, which is mostly correlational or conceptual.
