# Audit confirms most citations, flags over-attribution

Five verification notes checked 78 references cited in the v39, v35, Giriş ve Amaç and ITALL_düzenleme drafts. **Only one could not be found: Kokoç (2026, UEFAD 39[2]). It must be removed.** One more, Şat, Başaran & Özer (2026), exists but its authorship is unresolved. The rest exist, and their core bibliographic data match the drafts. The bigger risk is in how the drafts describe their sources. There are **13 high-priority misstatements**, and they share a pattern of over-attribution: a source is credited with a finer distinction or a stronger result than it reports. Examples:

- Cash et al.'s "seçici dikkat"
- Yan et al.'s "third evidence domain"
- Viberg et al.'s "four phases"
- Bastani et al.'s "düştü"
- Caplan's "kullanım sıklığı"
- Chan & Hu's "aşırı dayanma"

The most visible single error is statistical. Three drafts describe TÜİK's 19.2% as a share of recent internet users, but all coverage reports it as a share of **all individuals aged 16–74** ([AA](https://www.aa.com.tr/tr/bilim-teknoloji/turkiyede-uretken-yapay-zeka-kullandigini-beyan-edenlerin-orani-yuzde-19-2/3703970)).

Removing Kokoç leaves one sentence without verified support: that Turkish pre-service teachers report overreliance concerns. What can replace it is a group of verified Turkish studies on pre-service teachers' uses and concerns, plus three Turkish university-student studies. Those three link heavier GenAI use to higher dependency scores or poorer outcomes.

The notes also confirm **147 verified or partly verified sources**. That is enough for a 3–5-source parenthesis behind every key claim, from the addiction-versus-overreliance argument to the methods section.

Three limits of the checking apply to everything below:
- The research sessions could not reach doi.org, Crossref, TR Dizin or most full texts, so **every DOI still needs a Crossref check** before submission.
- Several numbers traced only to search snippets.
- **Five sources carry published corrections.**

## 1. Corrections to draft claims: 13 high-priority fixes among 56

Each item gives the draft location, the draft claim, the problem and the action. Priority tags: **[High]** changes meaning or would be caught by a reviewer; **[Medium]** is a precision problem; **[Low]** is housekeeping. Turkish suggested wordings come from the verification notes.

### A. Statistics and prevalence

1. **[High] TÜİK denominator.**

### C. Addiction, problematic use and dependence constructs

18. **[High] Griffiths overstated.**
    - Draft (v39 L10): "Klinik anlamdaki yapay zekâ bağımlılığı, büyük ölçüde Griffiths'in (2005)… modeline dayanır."
    - Problem: the four ChatGPT-addiction scales are "all framed after substance use disorder criteria". Only some go through the components model ([Ciudad-Fernández et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389698665_People_are_not_becoming_AIholic_Questioning_the_ChatGPT_addiction_construct)). "Klinik anlamdaki" also implies a recognised diagnosis, and none exists ([Vasiliu, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1816917/full)).
    - Action (rephrase): "…madde kullanım bozukluğu ölçütlerine ve bu ölçütlerden türetilen Griffiths'in (2005) bileşenler modeline dayanır". Drop "Klinik anlamdaki", or add that no formal diagnosis exists.
19. **[Medium] Ciudad-Fernández's "circular confirmatory approach".**
    - Draft (v39 L10): "Bu yönteme göre semptomlar önce varsayılır, sonra maddelerle doğrulanır."
    - Problem: that describes confirmatory logic in general. "Circular" means that items are recycled from online-addiction scales, which had themselves recycled substance-use items ([UNIL copy](https://api.unil.ch/iris/server/api/core/bitstreams/48569b25-9ee4-4284-ab38-f969e69b2f09/content)).
    - Action (rephrase): "Bu ölçekler, maddelerini mevcut çevrimiçi bağımlılık ölçeklerinden, onlar da maddelerini madde kullanım bozukluğu araçlarından devşirdiği için döngüsel bir doğrulayıcı yaklaşım izler."
20. **[High] Caplan's framework mischaracterised.**
    - Draft (v39 L11): "işlevsel bozulmayı merkeze alan daha yumuşak bir çerçeve".
    - Problem: GPIUS2 is a cognitive-behavioural model. Its centre is preference for online social interaction plus deficient self-regulation. Negative outcomes are the outcome variable, not the centre ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10654343/)).
    - Action (rephrase): "problemli kullanımı düzenleme güçlüğü ve bunun yol açtığı olumsuz sonuçlar üzerinden tanımlayan bilişsel-davranışçı bir çerçeve". Also say that deficient self-regulation *predicts* negative outcomes.
21. **[High] Caplan and "frequency".**
    - Draft (v39 L18): "Kullanım sıklığı ve kullanım süresinin uzaması … Caplan'ın (2010) modelinde düzenleme güçlüğü bileşenine karşılık gelir."
    - Problem: deficient self-regulation means cognitive preoccupation plus compulsive use. Frequency is not a GPIUS2 component ([PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0273895)).
    - Action (rephrase): "planlanandan uzun sürme ve kullanımı kontrol etmede güçlük … kompulsif kullanıma, dolayısıyla düzenleme güçlüğüne karşılık gelir; kullanım sıklığı ise modelde ayrı bir bileşen değildir."
22. **[High] v35 L175 parenthesis.**
    - Draft: "Görev koşulları ile değerlendirme çabası arasındaki ilişki farklı örneklemlerde de incelendi (Abbas vd., 2024; Lee vd., 2025; Zhang vd., 2024)."
    - Problem: none of the three is shown to have studied evaluation effort.
      - Zhang, Zhao et al. (2024) measured no evaluation or verification effort ([Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/77b8d71298ee5472a1bbf9ec3918946c/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
      - Abbas et al. (2024) linked workload and time pressure to ChatGPT *use* ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41239-024-00444-7)). This is an inference from the abstract summary; they did not study evaluation effort.
      - Lee et al.'s link to "zaman ve görev beklentileri" was not confirmed.
    - Action (rephrase): "görev koşulları (iş yükü, zaman baskısı, akademik stres) ile ÜYZ kullanımı ve bağımlılığı arasındaki ilişki (Abbas vd., 2024; Zhang, Zhao vd., 2024)". Keep Lee only after a full-text check.
23. **[Medium] Two "Zhang vd." papers.**
    - Problem: the AICD scale that Ciudad-Fernández cite as "Zhang et al., 2024" is Zhang, Yin, Zhang, Li & Li, *Cyberpsychology, Behavior, and Social Networking*, 28(2), 126–131 ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1089/cyber.2024.0240)). It is not the Korean IJETHE study.
    - Action: disambiguate as "Zhang, Zhao vd. (2024)" and "Zhang, Yin vd. (2025)". In English APA, use first-author initials (S. Zhang; X. Zhang).
24. **[Low] Kardefelt-Winther precision.**
    - Action: add "ve zaman içinde süreklilik" to the functional-impairment criterion. Cite exclusion criterion 3 at v39 L12, L185 and L191, next to Billieux and Ciudad-Fernández. Criterion 3: prolonged intensive involvement without significant impairment is not addiction ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1111/add.13763)).
25. **[Low] Oleas et al. (2026).**
    - Action: add "ve ölçme değişmezliği" to "iç yapı ve iç tutarlılığa ilişkin ilk kanıtlar". Do not describe item wording, which was not seen ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1882677/full)).

    - Action: add a parenthetical or footnote: "yanlış kullanım (akademik dürüstlük ihlali anlamında)".

## 3. Citation groups by claim: 44 parentheses built from verified sources

Rules for this section:

- Only sources marked VERIFIED or PARTLY VERIFIED in the notes appear here. Section 4 gives the unconfirmed element of each.
- In-text citations follow English APA 7. In Turkish, "et al." becomes "vd.".
- APA requires first-author initials where different first authors share a surname. That applies to C. vs I. Hou; H., S. S. Y. and T. W. Kim; H. vs T. H. Le; H. vs S. Zhang; J. L. vs L. O. Campbell. It also applies to Lee & See vs Lee et al., whose initials the notes did not record.
- **[TR]** marks Turkish-context studies. **[TR, inferred]** means the notes infer the setting from authorship.
- Each claim statement keeps the source's own hedges.

### 3.1 Prevalence and Türkiye data

**3.1.1 GenAI use is widespread among university students internationally.**
Global and single-country surveys show that most university students have used GenAI, and a systematic review describes ChatGPT as widely used in higher education. Reported rates range from 40.9% for academic tasks at one Italian university to 94% for assessed work among UK undergraduates. Because samples, timing and item wording differ, the rates are not directly comparable.
(Apata et al., 2026; Farinosi & Melchior, 2025; Jooste et al., 2025; Ravšelj et al., 2025; Stephenson & Armstrong, 2026)
- [Apata et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70309): review of 82 empirical studies (2022–Oct 2025); "widely used by students and faculty". *Systematic review.*
- [Farinosi & Melchior, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.70094): 531 Udine students plus 60 interviews; over two-thirds use ChatGPT for personal purposes, 40.9% for academic tasks. *Multi-method survey.*
- [Jooste et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/cae.70064): "widespread adoption" among South African engineering students; only 1% favour a ban. *Survey.*
- [Ravšelj et al., 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0315011): 23,218 students, 109 countries and territories, early 2024. *Global survey; correction published.*
- [Stephenson & Armstrong, 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/): 1,054 UK undergraduates, December 2025; 95% use AI, 94% for support with assessed work. *National survey report.*

**3.1.2 In Türkiye, GenAI use is concentrated among the young and already reaches education.**
In 2025, 19.2% of individuals aged 16–74 and 39.4% of 16–24-year-olds reported using GenAI, and 31.4% of users used it for formal education. In PISA 2025, 52% of Turkish 15-year-olds (OECD average 46%) reported using AI chatbots at least weekly to help them learn. A sample of Turkish university students held "dual-edged" views of AI-based education.
(Duran et al., 2025; OECD, 2026b; TÜİK, 2025)
- [TR] [Duran et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/berj.4188): 540 Turkish university students; enthusiasm plus ethics and isolation concerns. It gives *no prevalence estimate*. *Mixed methods.*
- [TR] [OECD, 2026b](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2025-results-volume-i-country-notes_2d4ff9ea-en/turkiye_3f140b0d-en.html): weekly AI-for-learning use among 15-year-olds. *International assessment; figures from index snippets.*
- [TR] [TÜİK, 2025](https://www.aa.com.tr/tr/bilim-teknoloji/turkiyede-uretken-yapay-zeka-kullandigini-beyan-edenlerin-orani-yuzde-19-2/3703970): Household ICT Usage Survey, the first GenAI item. *Official statistics.*

Rest the national figures on TÜİK and OECD alone. Duran et al. add context, not prevalence.

### 3.2 Pre-service teachers' dual role and Turkish studies

**3.2.1 Pre-service teachers meet GenAI as learners and as future teachers.**
Pre-service teachers engage with GenAI both as current learners and as future educators. Teacher-education work therefore treats AI literacy, professional judgement, and the risk of de-skilling or dependency as goals for teacher education.
(Bae et al., 2024; Khalid et al., 2026; Kohnke & Moorhouse, 2026; T. H. Le et al., 2026)
- [Bae et al., 2024](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1446841.pdf): pre-service teachers' views of GenAI for their own learning and for teaching ("dual perspectives"). *Empirical study of PST views; n not retrieved.*
- [Khalid et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.70547): 11 studies; AI as an assistant that does not replace teachers' professional judgement. *Systematic review.*
- [Kohnke & Moorhouse, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.70203): teachers "may offload professional work onto GenAI before developing evidence-informed pedagogies". *Practical guide / position piece.*
- [T. H. Le et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.70047): "double role as present learners and future educators"; gains in AI self-efficacy (pilot 14, evaluation 29). *Design-based research.*

**3.2.2 Turkish pre-service teachers mainly report educational uses, with accuracy, ethics and privacy concerns.**
Studies with pre-service teachers in Türkiye report mostly educational and practical chatbot use: lesson planning, homework, materials, assignments and presentations. Participants value time and effort savings and raise concerns about false information, ethics, privacy and bias. One study reports that some use was unethical.
(Dulkadir Yaman, 2025a, 2025b; Eroğlu & Ateş, 2025; Karataş & Yüce, 2024; Merzifonluoglu & Gunes, 2025)
- [TR] [Dulkadir Yaman, 2025a](https://doi.org/10.17943/etku.1485828): views on an AI text generator; false information, ethical issues, limited personalisation. *Qualitative views study.*
- [TR, inferred] [Dulkadir Yaman, 2025b](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.70277): chatbots used for educational, informational *and unethical* purposes. *Case study (interviews plus scales).*
- [TR] [Eroğlu & Ateş, 2025](https://doi.org/10.53629/sakaefd.1797431): Turkish-language teacher candidates used ChatGPT mostly for lesson planning and homework. *Qualitative; details from index listings.*
- [TR] [Karataş & Yüce, 2024](https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/7785): 141 pre-service teachers; three weeks of use; effects on professional identity and teaching approaches. *Narrative inquiry.*
- [TR, inferred] [Merzifonluoglu & Gunes, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.70053): 389 Gen Z pre-service teachers; ChatGPT used "as an advisor"; privacy, bias and security concerns. *Explanatory sequential mixed methods.*

No verified abstract in this group documents *overreliance* as a pre-service teacher concern. One index summary says that Eroğlu & Ateş's participants feared AI "could create addiction" ([DergiPark](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/sakaefd/article/1797431)). Confirm that in the full text before using it.

**3.2.3 Pre-service teachers often adapt rather than accept GenAI output, but evaluation quality varies.**
When pre-service teachers work with GenAI on lesson planning, many adapt its suggestions rather than accept them outright. Evaluation quality varies: higher-performing groups corroborated AI content with other sources, and lower-performing groups relied on it.
(Kalenda et al., 2025; Lehtinen et al., 2026; Merzifonluoglu & Gunes, 2025; Özüdoğru & Delen, 2026)
- [Kalenda et al., 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00472395241301388): 59 pre-service education students found missing details and inaccurate information in ChatGPT lesson plans. *Guided analysis.*
- [Lehtinen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70256): 75 pre-service teachers; corroboration vs repetitive prompting and reliance. *Video analysis plus process mining.*
- [TR, inferred] [Merzifonluoglu & Gunes, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.70053): often adapted AI suggestions to their own preferences. *Mixed methods.*
- [TR] [Özüdoğru & Delen, 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7102/16/9/1420): 25 pairs, 50 reflections; "seldom accepted GenAI recommendations outright". *Qualitative analysis of pair-authored reflections.*

**3.2.4 Among Turkish university students, heavier GenAI use goes with higher dependency scores and poorer outcomes.**
In Turkish university samples, more frequent or longer GenAI use is associated with higher dependency or "AI addiction" scores and, in one study, with poorer academic outcomes. The associations are correlational, and where strengths are reported they are weak.
(Bal et al., 2026; Barış Horzum et al., 2026; Gökçearslan et al., 2026)
- [TR] [Bal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.60077/medkul.1922767): 401 students; usage time and content creation relate positively to AI addiction, digital security negatively. *Survey with mediation.*
- [TR] [Barış Horzum et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/ijal.70153): 754 EMI students; "excessive time spent on GenAI platforms is correlated to poorer academic outcomes". *Survey.*
- [TR] [Gökçearslan et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1155/cad/2647537): 276 students; GenAI literacy correlates positively with dependency (r = .31); more frequent users score higher; correlations weak. *SEM survey.*

### 3.3 Addiction vs problematic use vs dependent use and overreliance

**3.3.1 Addiction-framed GenAI scales borrow their criteria from other addiction instruments.**
Scales of "ChatGPT/GenAI addiction" or dependence borrow criteria from substance-use-disorder and Internet-addiction instruments. Some borrow directly, some through DSM-5 Internet gaming disorder criteria, and some through Griffiths's components model. Some keep symptoms, such as withdrawal, that have not been studied empirically in GenAI use.
(Ciudad-Fernández et al., 2025; Goh et al., 2025; Griffiths, 2005; Hu et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2024)
- [Ciudad-Fernández et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2025.108325): four scales, all "framed after substance use disorder criteria". *Critical commentary.*
- [Goh et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2025.100845): three factors including *withdrawal*; six studies in the United States and Singapore. *Scale development.*
- [Griffiths, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1080/14659890500114359): six components (salience, mood modification, tolerance, withdrawal, conflict, relapse). *Theoretical paper.*
- [Hu et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2023.107760): PUCAI, with items adapted from a social-media addiction scale. *Survey.*
- [Yu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-024-06259-z): PCUS, 11 items based on DSM-5 IGD criteria. *Scale development.*

**3.3.2 Intensive use is not addiction without functional impairment.**
Addiction researchers argue that calling an everyday behaviour an addiction requires significant functional impairment or distress that persists over time. Prolonged intensive involvement without such impairment should not be conceptualised as addiction. According to Ciudad-Fernández et al., current studies of problematic ChatGPT use do not provide this evidence. Problematic GenAI chatbot use is not advanced as a formally established diagnostic entity.
(Billieux et al., 2015; Ciudad-Fernández et al., 2025; Kardefelt-Winther et al., 2017; Montag et al., 2025; Vasiliu, 2026)
- [Billieux et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1556/2006.4.2015.009): confirmatory research yields "an unlimited list of 'new' behavioral addictions". *Position paper.*
- [Ciudad-Fernández et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2025.108325): complaints such as laziness "hardly fit" clinically significant harm. *Critical commentary.*
- [Kardefelt-Winther et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.13763): operational definition; exclusion criterion 3. *Consensus/position paper.*
- [Montag et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.15337): critically examines "terms such as ChatGPT addiction". *Short communication; full text not read.*
- [Vasiliu, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1816917): not "a formally established diagnostic entity". *Narrative review.*

**3.3.3 Overreliance means accepting incorrect AI output.**
In human–AI interaction research, overreliance means accepting incorrect AI outputs; in automation terms this is "misuse". Appropriate reliance means accepting correct outputs and rejecting incorrect ones, avoiding both overreliance and underreliance.
(Parasuraman & Riley, 1997; Passi et al., 2024; Passi & Vorvoreanu, 2022; Schemmer et al., 2023; Zhai et al., 2024)
- [Parasuraman & Riley, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872097778543886): use, misuse, disuse, abuse; misuse = overreliance. *Conceptual review.*
- [Passi et al., 2024](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/appropriate-reliance-on-generative-ai-research-synthesis/): about 50 GenAI papers; appropriate reliance as the goal. *Research synthesis.*
- [Passi & Vorvoreanu, 2022](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/overreliance-on-ai-literature-review/): about 60 papers; overreliance defined as accepting incorrect outputs. *Literature review.*
- [Schemmer et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3581641.3584066): appropriateness of reliance (RAIR, RSR); 200 participants. *Conceptual paper plus experiment.*
- [Zhai et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40561-024-00316-7): 14 articles; overreliance affects decision-making, critical thinking and analytical reasoning. *Systematic review.*

**3.3.4 Reliance is not problematic in itself; what matters is who keeps control.**
Reliance on GenAI is not problematic in itself. Recent work locates the problem in whether learners keep judgement and control, and describes academic overreliance as cognitive and self-regulatory externalization rather than traditional technological addiction.
(C. Hou et al., 2025; T. W. Kim et al., 2025; Oleas et al., 2026; Wu & Lu, 2026)
- [C. Hou et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13613): strategy-graded reliance; "high trust in AI does not necessarily lead to overreliance". *Empirical problem-solving study.*
- [T. W. Kim et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/arcp.70008): cognitive labour × metacognitive oversight → "Cognitive Surrender"; the drift towards addiction is *hypothesised*. *Conceptual review.*
- [Oleas et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1882677): "cognitive and self-regulatory externalization … rather than a traditional form of technological addiction". *Scale development; internal-structure evidence only.*
- [Wu & Lu, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.70090): 342 undergraduates, three waves; higher-than-usual reliance predicted lower later perceived agency. *Longitudinal RI-CLPM.*

**3.3.5 Academic misconduct with GenAI is a separate construct.**
Academic-integrity research treats unauthorised GenAI use as cheating, plagiarism or fraud: a rule violation handled through policy, assessment design and detection. It is explained with its own frameworks, apart from dependence or self-regulation. No source was found that tests the discriminant validity of dependence against cheating.
(L. O. Campbell et al., 2025; Cotton et al., 2024; Kofinas et al., 2025; Perkins, 2023; Sangka et al., 2025)
- [L. O. Campbell et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/capr.12880): policies mostly call unauthorised use dishonesty or plagiarism. *Policy document analysis.*
- [Cotton et al., 2024](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1415334): detection difficulty and institutional strategies. *Discussion paper.*
- [Kofinas et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13585): markers at two UK universities could not tell GenAI-assisted work apart. *Experiments.*
- [Perkins, 2023](https://doi.org/10.53761/1.20.02.07): LLM text can evade software and trained staff. *Conceptual review.*
- [Sangka et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1155/hbe2/7118205): GPT-related fraud modelled with the fraud diamond. *Survey, Indonesia.*

**3.3.6 Turkish measurement relies on addiction-framed instruments.**
Turkish research on GenAI dependence has so far used addiction-framed instruments, two of them explicitly unidimensional. It reports levels and correlates. None of these studies separates dependent use from intensive functional use.
(Bal et al., 2026; Kılıç & Koca, 2025; Maral et al., 2025; Savaş, 2024)
- [TR] [Bal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.60077/medkul.1922767): AI addiction relatively low; digital-competence subdimensions predict it. *Survey.*
- [TR] [Kılıç & Koca, 2025](https://doi.org/10.48146/odusobiad.1725693): 424 Faculty of Education students measured with Savaş's scale; results not retrieved. *Survey.*
- [TR] [Maral et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11469-025-01509-y): PCGUS, 9 items, one factor, measurement invariance. *Scale validation.*
- [TR] [Savaş, 2024](https://doi.org/10.56639/jsar.1509301): adaptation of the DIA; 584 students; unidimensional. *Scale adaptation.*

### 3.4 Cognitive offloading

**3.4.1 Offloading is normal; whether it harms depends on design and use.**
Cognitive offloading, using physical action or external aids to reduce a task's cognitive demands, is a normal and metacognitively guided strategy. With AI, whether it harms depends on design and use. It can block the acquisition, or speed the decay, of practised skills, while basic cognitive abilities appear resistant to change.
(Cash et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026; Risko & Gilbert, 2016)
- [Cash et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2026.06.004): distinguishes specific learned skills from basic abilities. *Forum piece; content from secondary coverage.*
- [Li et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100571): 67 studies; scaffolded designs help, unstructured use "often triggered cognitive offloading". *Systematic review.*
- [Risko & Gilbert, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002): definition; the decision to offload is metacognitively driven. *Theoretical review.*

**3.4.2 Offloading to LLMs lowers effort and can reduce depth and memory.**
Offloading raises immediate performance but lowers memory for the offloaded information. With LLMs, learners invest less effort and report lower cognitive load, but give weaker justifications and gain shallower knowledge. The evidence is mostly single-session or correlational.
(Farhat, 2026; Gerlich, 2025; Grinschgl et al., 2021; Melumad & Yun, 2025; Stadler et al., 2024)
- [Farhat, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.70205): self-reported memory decline and less critical thinking under over-reliance. *Qualitative interviews.*
- [Gerlich, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006): 666 participants; frequent AI use relates negatively to critical thinking, mediated by offloading. *Cross-sectional; correction published.*
- [Grinschgl et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/17470218211008060): offloading boosts performance and diminishes memory (non-AI). *Experiment.*
- [Melumad & Yun, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf316): seven experiments; LLM learners learned more shallowly and wrote shorter, less original advice. *Experiments.*
- [Stadler et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2024.108386): 91 students; lower load, weaker justifications. *Randomised experiment.*

**3.4.3 Designs that protect learner effort reduce harm.**
Designs that preserve the learner's own effort reduce or avoid the negative effects of unrestricted use. Examples include hint-only tutors, note-taking, generating ideas before consulting ChatGPT, metacognitive support and scaffolded inquiry.
(Bastani et al., 2025; Kreijkes et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026; Wong & Qiu, 2026; Xu et al., 2025)
- [TR] [Bastani et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122): in the GPT Tutor (hints) condition, harm was "largely mitigated"; nearly 1,000 students in a Turkish high school. *Cluster-randomised field experiment.*
- [Kreijkes et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2025.105514): 405 students aged 14–15; note-taking (alone or with the LLM) beat the LLM alone at three days. *Randomised classroom experiment.*
- [Li et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100571): recommends reflective prompts and rubric-guided evaluation. *Systematic review.*
- [Wong & Qiu, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10118-7): 196 students; "think first" beat unguided AI on later independent originality. *Experiment.*
- [Xu et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13599): metacognitive support improved all six SRL dimensions; unsupported GenAI users declined in five. *Experiment.*

### 3.5 Metacognition, self-regulation and help seeking

**3.5.1 GenAI makes metacognitive demands and can take over regulation.**
Using GenAI demands metacognition: setting goals, prompting, judging outputs, and deciding when to use the tool at all. Experimental and trace-data studies suggest that, without support, GenAI can take over monitoring and evaluation from learners. So far, "metacognitive laziness" has been inferred from process patterns, not measured directly.
(Darvishi et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2025; Iqbal et al., 2026; Tankelevitch et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025)
- [Darvishi et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104967): 1,625 students "rely on, rather than learn from" AI prompts in a peer-feedback platform (not a chatbot). *Field study.*
- [Fan et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13544): 117 students; GenAI "may also take over (offload) regulation". *Randomised lab study.*
- [Iqbal et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70300): 87 participants; constant support "may prevent students from … developing their own internal metacognitive judgement". *Trace data, Markov models.*
- [Tankelevitch et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642902): metacognitive demands at prompting, evaluating and relying on output, and workflow strategy. *Conceptual paper.*
- [Xu et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13599): SRL declines without metacognitive support. *Experiment.*

**3.5.2 Regulation can be shared or scaffolded rather than lost.**
Regulation is not simply lost to AI. Hybrid and co-regulation views describe control moving among the learner, teachers, peers and AI, and metacognitive scaffolds can keep learners regulating their own work.
(Lodge et al., 2023; Molenaar, 2022; Xu et al., 2025)
- [Lodge et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.53761/1.20.7.02): GenAI within a network of co-regulation. *Commentary.*
- [Molenaar, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.12527): hybrid human–AI learning technologies. *Conceptual paper.*
- [Xu et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13599): scaffolds restore SRL. *Experiment.*

**3.5.3 Help seeking is a self-regulated process shaped by goals.**
Help seeking is a self-regulated, multi-step process, from recognising a need to processing the help received. Help that serves learning (instrumental or autonomous) differs from help that only finishes the task (executive or expedient). Learners' goals shape which occurs, and this holds when the help comes from GenAI.
(Karabenick, 2004; Karabenick & Dembo, 2011; Nelson-Le Gall, 1981, 1985; Wang et al., 2026)
- [Karabenick, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.96.3.569): approach (autonomous) vs avoidance (expedient) patterns (N = 883; N = 852). *Two survey studies.*
- [Karabenick & Dembo, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1002/tl.442): eight-step self-regulated help-seeking process. *Conceptual paper.*
- [Nelson-Le Gall, 1981](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0273229781900198): help seeking as a problem-solving skill; origin of the instrumental/executive distinction. *Theoretical review.*
- [Nelson-Le Gall, 1985](https://doi.org/10.3102/0091732X012001055): help-seeking behaviour in learning. *Review.*
- [Wang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.70059): mastery-oriented learners link GenAI requests more closely to their SRL. *Empirical log/SRL study.*

**3.5.4 GenAI is changing where students seek help.**
University students increasingly go to GenAI first, especially for quick, well-defined problems, because of speed and low social cost. For some purposes they still prefer people. Qualitative evidence suggests the shift can crowd out peer help.
(I. Hou, Man, et al., 2025; I. Hou, Mettille, et al., 2024; H. Le et al., 2025; Viberg et al., 2026)
- [I. Hou, Man, et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3724363.3729024): 17 interviews at seven R1 universities; peers redirect questions to ChatGPT; students feel isolated. *Qualitative.*
- [I. Hou, Mettille, et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3636243.3636248): ChatGPT compared with human help sources on quality, latency and trustworthiness. *Survey, computing students.*
- [H. Le et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13614): 114 students; preference for ChatGPT rose after exposure; authors warn of over-reliance. *Experiment.*
- [Viberg et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.20851/ll.v8.60): 20 Swedish STEM students; help seeking "layered and context-dependent". *Qualitative interviews.*

### 3.6 Evaluative judgement, verification, trust and fluency

**3.6.1 Evaluative judgement extends to judging GenAI outputs and processes.**
Evaluative judgement is the capability to make decisions about the quality of one's own and others' work. It involves understanding quality standards and judging specific instances. With GenAI it extends to judging the outputs and the processes that produce them.
(Bearman & Ajjawi, 2023; Bearman et al., 2024; Naidoo et al., 2021; Tai et al., 2018)
- [Bearman & Ajjawi, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13337): pedagogy for AI as a "black box"; building judgement in weighing AI's contribution. *Conceptual paper.*
- [Bearman et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2335321): three foci — outputs, processes, and GenAI assessing students' judgement. *Conceptual paper.*
- [Naidoo et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.13268): the two components of evaluative judgement. *Educational conceptual paper.*
- [Tai et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0220-3): the definition (p. 471). *Conceptual paper.*

**3.6.2 Students often accept incorrect GenAI output; corroboration marks stronger performers.**
Evidence beyond self-report shows that students often accept incorrect GenAI output. ChatGPT assistance did not reduce the inclusion of wrong content, and nearly half of ChatGPT-supported physics solutions were wrongly judged correct. LLM users also gave weaker justifications. Among pre-service teachers, corroborating AI content with other sources distinguished the stronger groups.
(Krupp et al., 2024; Lehtinen et al., 2026; Stadler et al., 2024; Urban et al., 2025)
- [Krupp et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3233/FAIA240195): "unreflected acceptance" (N = 12 vs 27). *Small experiment.*
- [Lehtinen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70256): high-performing pre-service teacher groups corroborated AI content. *Process mining.*
- [Stadler et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2024.108386): weaker justifications with an LLM. *Randomised experiment.*
- [Urban et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13591): wrong content not reduced; students with a higher self-reported need to evaluate included *more* inaccuracies. *Experiment.*

**3.6.3 Trust differs from reliance, and confidence cuts both ways.**
Trust is an attitude and reliance is a behaviour; appropriate reliance needs trust calibrated to what the system can actually do. In self-reports, higher confidence in GenAI goes with less critical thinking and higher self-confidence with more. High trust does not necessarily produce overreliance.
(C. Hou et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2025; Lee & See, 2004; Nazaretsky et al., 2022)
- [C. Hou et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13613): critical thinking linked to reflective and cautious use. *Empirical study.*
- [Lee et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713778): 319 knowledge workers, 936 examples. *Survey.*
- [Lee & See, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392): trust definition (p. 54); trust vs reliance. *Theoretical review.*
- [Nazaretsky et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13232): explaining how the AI decides raised K-12 science teachers' trust; teacher agency to "override". *Professional development study.*

**3.6.4 Fluent, confident LLM language inflates trust.**
Fluent statements are judged more likely to be true. Under default explanations, users overestimate LLM accuracy, and longer explanations raise confidence without raising accuracy. First-person uncertainty expressions reduce agreement with the system. Urban et al. suggest that students treat ChatGPT as an epistemic authority because of its authoritative tone.
(Alter & Oppenheimer, 2009; S. S. Y. Kim et al., 2024; Steyvers et al., 2025; Urban et al., 2025)
- [Alter & Oppenheimer, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868309341564): fluency as a metacognitive cue to truth. *Integrative review.*
- [S. S. Y. Kim et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3630106.3658941): "I'm not sure, but…" lowered agreement and raised accuracy. *Large experiment.*
- [Steyvers et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00976-7): calibration and discrimination gaps. *Experiments.*
- [Urban et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13591): "authoritative tone and human-like interaction". *Experiment; practitioner notes.*

**3.6.5 Explanations alone do not cure overreliance; uncertainty cues help.**
Simple explanations do not reduce overreliance on AI and may increase it. Interventions that prompt reflection have been tested, and uncertainty expressions and confidence-calibrated explanations reduced overreliance.
(Buçinca et al., 2021; S. S. Y. Kim et al., 2024; Steyvers et al., 2025)
- [Buçinca et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287): three cognitive-forcing designs vs simple explanations (N = 199); the result details were not re-checked. *Experiment.*
- [S. S. Y. Kim et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3630106.3658941): first-person uncertainty reduced overreliance. *Experiment.*
- [Steyvers et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00976-7): explanations matched to model confidence narrowed both gaps. *Experiments.*

**3.6.6 Teacher education should make verification an explicit practice.**
Teacher-education research and reviews recommend making verification and critical appraisal of GenAI output explicit. Suggested forms include tasks that require justifying and correcting AI output, low-stakes use with structured reflection, and AI-literacy frameworks that keep professional judgement with the teacher. In a cross-country comparison, Turkish pre-service teachers scored lower than German peers on critical appraisal.
(Aksoy et al., 2026; Khalid et al., 2026; Kohnke & Moorhouse, 2026; Lehtinen et al., 2026; Su et al., 2026)
- [TR] [Aksoy et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.70454): 605 pre-service teachers in Türkiye and Germany; SNAIL; German participants higher, especially in critical appraisal. *Cross-cultural survey.*
- [Khalid et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.70547): human-centred, ethically informed AI literacy. *Systematic review.*
- [Kohnke & Moorhouse, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.70203): low-stakes use, scenario-based microteaching, structured reflection. *Practical guide.*
- [Lehtinen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70256): recommends verifying GenAI information against other sources. *Process mining.*
- [Su et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70241): 54 studies; 46.3% raised accuracy concerns; verification as a core practice. *Systematic review.*

**3.6.7 AI literacy includes evaluating AI.**
AI-literacy frameworks list critical evaluation of AI as a component separate from knowing about AI and using it.
(Aksoy et al., 2026; Long & Magerko, 2020; Ng et al., 2021)
- [TR] [Aksoy et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.70454): critical-appraisal dimension. *Survey.*
- [Long & Magerko, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376727): AI literacy includes the ability to "critically evaluate AI". *Conceptual paper.*
- [Ng et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2021.100041): "evaluate and create" as one of four aspects (Bloom-style, not fact-checking). *Exploratory review of 30 articles.*

### 3.7 Perceived competence and skill decay

**3.7.1 Perceived competence is not measured skill.**
Access to external knowledge can inflate people's estimates of what they themselves know. AI assistance may hide skill decay or non-acquisition from the user. GenAI can raise creative self-efficacy while unsettling ownership and agency. Evidence specifically on GenAI and perceived competence is mostly conceptual or self-reported.
(Fisher et al., 2015; Habib & Gardiner, 2026; Lee et al., 2025; Macnamara et al., 2024)
- [Fisher et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000070): nine experiments; Internet search inflates self-rated knowledge (web search, not GenAI). *Experiments.*
- [Habib & Gardiner, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tsc.2026.102234): higher creative self-efficacy, disrupted agency and ownership. *Systematic review; study counts unverified.*
- [Lee et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713778): self-confidence associated with more critical thinking. *Survey.*
- [Macnamara et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-024-00572-8): decay and non-acquisition "without performers' awareness"; examples are radiology and surgery. *Theoretical paper.*

**3.7.2 Self-efficacy, agency and reliance are linked.**
Cross-sectional studies link academic self-efficacy with AI reliance or dependence, directly or through stress and expectations. In one longitudinal study, higher-than-usual reliance predicted lower later perceived agency.
(Elzeky et al., 2026; Wu & Lu, 2026; S. Zhang et al., 2024)
- [Elzeky et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1155/nuf/3301894): among nursing students, ChatGPT reliance was associated with lower self-efficacy, less self-directed learning and more problem-solving difficulty. *Cross-sectional.*
- [Wu & Lu, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.70090): reliance → lower later agency; the reverse path was not supported. *Longitudinal.*
- [S. Zhang et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-024-00467-0): 300 Korean students; the self-efficacy–dependency link runs through stress and performance expectations; the direct path was not significant. *Cross-sectional survey.*

**3.7.3 Assisted performance is not learning.**
In randomised and quasi-experimental studies, GenAI raises output quality or practice performance while it is available. Unassisted, delayed or transfer outcomes stay the same or fall, unless the design protects the learner's own effort.
(Bastani et al., 2025; Fan et al., 2025; Kreijkes et al., 2026; Melanou et al., 2026; Yan, Greiff, et al., 2025)
- [TR] [Bastani et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122): +48% and +127% in practice; −17% on the unassisted exam vs control (GPT Base). *Field experiment.*
- [Fan et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13544): essay scores rose; no difference in knowledge gain or transfer. *Randomised lab study.*
- [Kreijkes et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2025.105514): the LLM alone was worst at three days. *Classroom RCT.*
- [Melanou et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70322): a semester, N = 87; knowledge gains in all groups, "no additional benefits of AI integration". *Quasi-experiment.*
- [Yan, Greiff, et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-025-00467-5): do not read assisted performance as learning. *Comment.*
- *Counterpoint for balance:* [Deng et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2024.105224). This meta-analysis of 69 experiments reports positive effects on performance. As the special-section editorial notes, such meta-analyses mostly measure performance outcomes ([Yan, Pammer-Schindler, et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/ai/10.1111/bjet.70000)). Because two Yan 2025 works are cited, APA requires the second author in every citation of either.

**3.7.4 Skill atrophy is a theoretical risk, not yet longitudinal evidence.**
Theoretical work proposes that AI assistance can raise task performance while human skill stalls or declines (a "performance mirage"), and that intermittent practice without AI may preserve skill. It also argues that specific practised skills are more vulnerable than basic cognitive abilities. These are models and arguments, not longitudinal evidence.
(Cash et al., 2026; Ganuthula et al., 2026; Macnamara et al., 2024)
- [Cash et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2026.06.004): skill decay vs basic abilities. *Forum piece.*
- [Ganuthula et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1155/hbe2/8303770): "Paradox of Augmentation"; simulations. *Theoretical/mathematical model.*
- [Macnamara et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-024-00572-8): accelerated decay and hindered acquisition. *Theoretical paper.*

### 3.8 Method: design, analysis, rigour, validity and AI use

**3.8.1 Qualitative description.**
Qualitative description aims at a comprehensive, low-inference summary of events in participants' everyday terms. It still involves interpretation, and its credibility depends on transparent, consistent analytic decisions.
(Bradshaw et al., 2017; Doyle et al., 2020; H. Kim et al., 2017; Sandelowski, 2000, 2010)
- [Bradshaw et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/2333393617742282): suited "where information is required directly from those experiencing the phenomenon". *Methods paper.*
- [Doyle et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1744987119880234): "simplicity, flexibility and utility"; critiques of rigour. *Methods overview.*
- [H. Kim et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/nur.21768): 55 studies; 91% did not report positionality. *Systematic review.*
- [Sandelowski, 2000](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/1098-240X(200008)23:4%3C334::AID-NUR9%3E3.0.CO;2-G): the foundational definition. *Methods essay.*
- [Sandelowski, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/nur.20362): corrects the misconception that qualitative description "requires no interpretation". *Methods essay.*

**3.8.2 Theory-guided qualitative description and framework-based coding of open-ended surveys.**
Theoretical frameworks may guide qualitative descriptive studies, including their analysis. Open-ended survey responses have been content-analysed with combined deductive (framework-based) and inductive coding.
(Henderson et al., 2024; H. Kim et al., 2017; Villamin et al., 2025)
- [Henderson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/01612840.2024.2318603): deductive and inductive coding of open-ended community-survey responses. *Content analysis.*
- [H. Kim et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/nur.21768): frameworks guide qualitative descriptive studies. *Systematic review.*
- [Villamin et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jan.16481): frameworks may guide the interview guide, data collection, analysis or discussion. *Worked-example guide.*

**3.8.3 Directed content analysis.**
In directed (deductive) qualitative content analysis, prior theory or research supplies the initial codes. These are applied through a structured or unconstrained categorisation matrix and combined with inductive category development.
(Assarroudi et al., 2018; Elo & Kyngäs, 2008; Hsieh & Shannon, 2005; Mayring, 2000)
- [Assarroudi et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/1744987117741667): a 16-step process. *Methods paper.*
- [Elo & Kyngäs, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2648.2007.04569.x): "structured or unconstrained matrix". *Methods paper.*
- [Hsieh & Shannon, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/1049732305276687): theory as "guidance for initial codes". *Methods paper.*
- [Mayring, 2000](https://doi.org/10.17169/fqs-1.2.1089): deductive category application plus inductive development. *Methods paper.*

**3.8.4 Meaning units, manifest and latent content, abstraction.**
Content analysis separates manifest from latent content and works with meaning units: words, sentences or paragraphs related by content and context. The results range in abstraction and interpretation from categories and descriptive themes to themes of meaning.
(Elo & Kyngäs, 2008; Graneheim et al., 2017; Graneheim & Lundman, 2004)
- [Elo & Kyngäs, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2648.2007.04569.x): preparation, organising and reporting phases. *Methods paper.*
- [Graneheim et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nedt.2017.06.002): "descriptive themes" vs "themes of meaning"; supports v39's use of "tema". *Discussion paper.*
- [Graneheim & Lundman, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nedt.2003.10.001): meaning unit, condensation, manifest vs latent content. *Methods paper.*

**3.8.5 Counting in qualitative research.**
Counts have legitimate roles in qualitative research: describing samples, making "some" and "most" precise, and checking interpretations. The sources warn against verbal, excessive, misleading or acontextual counting. Using numbers does not by itself make a study mixed methods.
(Hannah & Lautsch, 2011; Maxwell, 2010; Sandelowski, 2001)
- [Hannah & Lautsch, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/1056492610375988): when to count, when to avoid it, when to "closet" it. *Methods essay.*
- [Maxwell, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/1077800410364740): "quasi-statistics". *Methods essay.*
- [Sandelowski, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1002/nur.1025): numbers "document, verify, and test" interpretations. *Methods essay.*

**3.8.6 Percent agreement ignores chance.**
Percent agreement does not correct for chance agreement, so chance-corrected indices such as Cohen's kappa are recommended alongside it. Reporting intercoder reliability supports transparency, although its place in qualitative research is debated.
(Bıkmaz Bilgen & Doğan, 2017; Hallgren, 2012; McHugh, 2012; O'Connor & Joffe, 2020)
- [TR] [Bıkmaz Bilgen & Doğan, 2017](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/epod/article/294847): compares kappa, log-linear models and Krippendorff's alpha (50 students, 10 raters). *Comparative measurement study.*
- [Hallgren, 2012](https://doi.org/10.20982/tqmp.08.1.p023): prefer kappa or ICC to percent agreement. *Tutorial; erratum exists.*
- [McHugh, 2012](https://doi.org/10.11613/BM.2012.031): percent agreement's "inability to account for chance agreement". *Methods review.*
- [O'Connor & Joffe, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1609406919899220): the debate for and against intercoder reliability, with guidelines. *Methods guideline.*

**3.8.7 Independent coding and consensus.**
Team coding can combine independent coding, agreement checks and discussion to reach consensus. Standardising the units that coders compare (unitisation) is a precondition for meaningful agreement. The drafts' binary per-response decision already satisfies this; say so.
(J. L. Campbell et al., 2013; MacPhail et al., 2016; O'Connor & Joffe, 2020)
- [J. L. Campbell et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0049124113500475): the unitisation problem. *Methods paper.*
- [MacPhail et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/1468794115577012): process guidelines for group coding. *Methods paper.*
- [O'Connor & Joffe, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1609406919899220): reflexive team dialogue. *Methods guideline.*

**3.8.8 Trustworthiness across all phases.**
Trustworthiness in content analysis covers credibility, dependability, confirmability, transferability and authenticity. It must be addressed in preparation, organisation and reporting, not only through coder agreement. In Turkish usage, "inandırıcılık" names credibility specifically.
(Arslan, 2022; Başkale, 2016; Elo et al., 2014; Graneheim & Lundman, 2004)
- [TR] [Arslan, 2022](https://doi.org/10.30794/pausbed.1116878): inandırıcılık, aktarılabilirlik, tutarlılık, doğrulanabilirlik. *Turkish methods paper.*
- [TR] [Başkale, 2016](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/deuhfed/article/586804): validity, reliability and sample size in qualitative research (per its title; content not summarised in the notes). *Turkish methods paper.*
- [Elo et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/2158244014522633): trustworthiness in every phase. *Methods paper.*
- [Graneheim & Lundman, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nedt.2003.10.001): measures to achieve trustworthiness. *Methods paper.*

**3.8.9 Advantages of qualitative surveys.**
Fully qualitative surveys that prioritise qualitative research values and follow a rigorous design can produce rich data on groups' experiences and practices. They have practical advantages, especially online.
(Braun et al., 2021; Terry & Braun, 2017; Tombs & Strange, 2024)
- [Braun et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/13645579.2020.1805550): the online survey as a qualitative tool. *Methods paper.*
- [Terry & Braun, 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/collecting-qualitative-data/short-but-often-sweet/AA3A8DCE11280541BC454A7BC5880955): "short but often sweet". *Book chapter.*
- [Tombs & Strange, 2024](https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.1102): rich findings when designed rigorously. *Methods paper.*

**3.8.10 Limits of qualitative surveys.**
Free-text survey answers are often too thin for robust stand-alone qualitative insight, and methods literature rarely discusses how to analyse them. Open-ended questions alone do not make a study qualitative.
(Decorte et al., 2019; LaDonna et al., 2018; Tombs & Strange, 2024)
- [Decorte et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/2059799119825606): feedback comments are "rarely discussed in methodological literature". *Methods reflection.*
- [LaDonna et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1097/ACM.0000000000002088): answers "rarely produce data rich enough". *Invited commentary.*
- [Tombs & Strange, 2024](https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.1102): warns against assuming that open questions make research qualitative. *Methods paper.*

**3.8.11 Qualitative input from the target population informs domain definition and content validity.**
Instrument development starts from a clearly defined construct domain. Qualitative input from the target population, such as interviews and focus groups, can inform domain definition and item generation and strengthen content validity. Behavioural-addiction researchers recommend such person-centred qualitative work before items are written.
(Boateng et al., 2018; Clark & Watson, 2019; Haynes et al., 1995; Kardefelt-Winther et al., 2017; Vogt et al., 2004)
- [Boateng et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2018.00149): domain identification; inductive item generation; cognitive interviews. *Primer.*
- [Clark & Watson, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/pas0000626): construct validity as the goal of scale development. *Methods review.*
- [Haynes et al., 1995](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Content-validity-in-psychological-assessment:-A-to-Haynes-Richard/402f0f1eca459139d141eeac5298958fd7557483): content validity is conditional and dynamic; qualitative and quantitative methods. *Methods review.*
- [Kardefelt-Winther et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.13763): "Only in a second step should items be developed". *Position paper.*
- [Vogt et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/1040-3590.16.3.231): focus groups inform "multiple stages of instrument development". *Methods paper.*

**3.8.12 Validity evidence based on test content.**
Validity is the degree to which evidence and theory support proposed score interpretations. Evidence based on test content covers domain definition, domain representation, domain relevance, and the appropriateness of construction procedures.
(American Educational Research Association et al., 2014; Haynes et al., 1995; Sireci & Faulkner-Bond, 2014)
- [AERA et al., 2014](https://www.aera.net/publications/books/standards-for-educational-psychological-testing-2014-edition): the definition of validity. *Professional standards.*
- [Haynes et al., 1995](https://www.scirp.org/reference/referencespapers?referenceid=1070970): elements of content validity. *Methods review.*
- [Sireci & Faulkner-Bond, 2014](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/4167.pdf): the four aspects. *Methods paper.*

**3.8.13 AI-assisted qualitative analysis.**
LLMs such as ChatGPT do reasonably well at descriptive coding but are weaker at subtle interpretive themes. Their themes overlap only partly with those of human coders, so they need human verification. Understanding of their potential is still early.
(Hamilton et al., 2023; Hitch, 2024; Morgan, 2023; H. Zhang et al., 2025)
- [Hamilton et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/16094069231201504): human and ChatGPT themes overlapped only partly. *Comparative study.*
- [Hitch, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/10497323231217392): potential "remains in its infancy". *Methods discussion.*
- [Morgan, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/16094069231211248): "less successful at locating subtle, interpretive themes". *Re-analysis case study.*
- [H. Zhang et al., 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949882125000283): 17 interviews and a prompt framework built with 13 analysts. *Qualitative / design study.*

**3.8.14 Disclosing AI use.**
Researchers should disclose, describe and explain their use of AI tools, including the tools' limitations, and they remain responsible for analytic decisions.
(Christou, 2023; Hosseini et al., 2023; Resnik & Hosseini, 2025)
- [Christou, 2023](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/tqr/vol28/iss7/9/): "five key considerations" for rigorous use. *Methods review.*
- [Hosseini et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/17470161231180449): ethics of disclosing AI use in writing. *Ethics paper.*
- [Resnik & Hosseini, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-024-00493-8): "disclose, describe, and explain … including its limitations". *Ethics paper.*

## Conclusion

The central argument of the article is stronger in the literature than the drafts currently show. The claim is that dependent use is defined by what is delegated and how it is evaluated, not by frequency, and that it is not clinical addiction. The support comes from three levels:

- Kardefelt-Winther et al.'s exclusion criterion 3 states the rule: intensive use without impairment is not addiction.
- Ciudad-Fernández et al. apply that rule to GenAI scales.
- Oleas et al., C. Hou et al. and Wu & Lu offer empirical alternatives, such as externalization and the question of who keeps control.

Two Turkish findings turn the gap in local measurement into an argument for the study. Gökçearslan et al. found that *literacy* correlates positively with "dependency". Bal et al. found that usage time and content creation raise "AI addiction" scores. Addiction-framed Turkish instruments may therefore be capturing competent, intensive use, which is exactly the confusion the article's qualitative dimensions separate. This is an inference from the direction of the reported effects, not the original authors' interpretation.

The weakest evidence is where the article is most local. After Kokoç is removed, no verified source shows that Turkish pre-service teachers themselves voice overreliance concerns. Eroğlu & Ateş (2025) is the only verified lead. The article's own finding, that only 4 of 41 candidates consulted an external source, is itself new evidence on that point, and it fits Aksoy et al.'s critical-appraisal gap between Turkish and German pre-service teachers.

For the remaining unverified numbers ("42/14", "altı öğretmen", "6.311", "44%"), deleting is cheaper and safer than defending. The work left before submission is mechanical: run a Crossref sweep of every DOI, run the TR Dizin queries listed in the notes, and read the full texts of the dozen sources whose finer claims were checked only against abstracts.
